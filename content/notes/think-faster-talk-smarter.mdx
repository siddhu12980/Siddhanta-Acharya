## Think Faster, Talk Smarter

I recently watched a talk by Matt Abrahams, a Stanford lecturer, on how to communicate better in spontaneous situations like cold calls, small talk, feedback, interviews, or unexpected questions.

The main idea was simple: speaking well in the moment is not about being naturally perfect. It is about managing your mindset and using simple structures to organize your thoughts.

## 1. Manage Anxiety

Spontaneous speaking often feels difficult because anxiety takes up mental space.

A few useful ways to reduce it:

* Take deep belly breaths, with a longer exhale than inhale.
* Bring yourself into the present moment.
* Use small warmups like tongue twisters before speaking.
* Focus less on the fear of a future mistake and more on the current conversation.

## 2. Maximize Mediocrity

One useful mindset shift is to stop trying to give the perfect answer every time.

When we constantly judge ourselves while speaking, we use extra mental energy. Giving yourself permission to simply answer helps you think more clearly.

## 3. See Communication as an Opportunity

Instead of seeing unexpected communication as a threat, treat it as a chance to connect, learn, or contribute.

Some helpful ideas:

* **Not yet**: You may not be good at it yet, but you can improve.
* **Yes, and**: Build on the conversation instead of blocking it.
* **Next play**: Move on quickly after mistakes.
* **Missed takes**: Treat mistakes as chances to improve next time.

## 4. Listen Deeply

Good spontaneous speaking starts with better listening.

Instead of listening only to reply, listen to understand. Give the other person space, ask clarifying questions, and paraphrase what they said. This also gives you time to form a better response.

## 5. Use Structure

Structure helps avoid rambling.

One simple framework is:

**What? → So what? → Now what?**

* **What:** What is the main point?
* **So what:** Why does it matter?
* **Now what:** What should happen next?

This is useful for answers, feedback, updates, and explanations.

## 6. Stay Focused and Concise

Before speaking, be clear about your goal.

Ask yourself what you want to create:

* **Information:** What should they know?
* **Emotion:** What should they feel?
* **Action:** What should they do?

A useful pitching structure is:

**What if you could... → So that... → For example... → And that’s not all...**

## Key Takeaway

Spontaneous communication is a skill that improves with practice. The goal is not to sound perfect, but to become calmer, clearer, and more structured in the moment.

The best way to improve is through repetition, reflection, and feedback.
